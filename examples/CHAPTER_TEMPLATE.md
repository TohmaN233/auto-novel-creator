# Chapter Template — Fantasy-SciFi

> This template works alongside a custom style guide file (if configured).
> Structure is governed by the template; prose voice is governed by the style file.

---

## Per-Chapter Targets

- **Word count**: 8000 words (allowed range: 6000–10000)
- **POV**: Third-person limited — narration stays within the POV character's knowledge boundary
- **Tense**: Past tense (primary); past perfect for flashbacks/inserts

---

## Chapter Structure

### 1. Opening Hook (1–2 paragraphs, ~500 words)

- Drop directly into the scene — no "Character X woke up that morning" openings
- Anchor the reader with a **concrete detail** or **unresolved question**
- Optional: use an in-universe document excerpt as a chapter epigraph

### 2. Scene Development (60–70% of chapter)

- **Information release rhythm** (core loop):
  ```
  Worldbuilding question → Character asks → Knowledgeable character explains →
  Protagonist internalizes & reflects → New confusion arises
  ```
- Dialogue carries heavy exposition and ideological conflict
- Dialogue tags are minimal: "X said", "X replied with a smile" — no adverb abuse
- Internal monologue is woven between dialogue lines without "she thought" tags
- Worldbuilding emerges through action and consequence, not info-dumps
- Every scene must advance **both** plot **and** character/thematic depth

### 3. Mid-Chapter Turn (optional, ~1000 words)

- A revelation, reversal, or decision point
- The protagonist questions something about the established rules — a logical "something doesn't add up"

### 4. Chapter Closing (1–3 paragraphs, ~500 words)

- **Ending rule**: close with an intellectual cliffhanger or unresolved question
- Not an action-based cliff-hanger, but a cognitive gap
- Example tone: "He didn't answer. But the question remained, like a thorn that wouldn't dissolve."

---

## Scene Transitions

- Use `---` dividers for shifts in time / location / POV
- Bridge gaps with a sentence or two summarizing character state changes — no daily routine filler
- Slow down for important scenes: allow extended dialogue and introspection
- Every paragraph must justify its existence — if removing it changes nothing for the reader, cut it

---

## Emotional Expression

- **Never write directly**: "She cried" / "He felt sad"
- **Action side-channel**: "Tears blurred her vision before she could blink them away"
- **Environmental reflection**: "The air around them seemed to grow heavier"
- **Self-rationalization**: "Well, it's not like it matters anyway."
- Cool, restrained, understated — let the reader feel the temperature between the lines

---

## Internal Monologue

- Self-interrogation style: stack questions to build a contemplative rhythm
- Use quoted keywords for precise definitions or ironic emphasis
- Don't give the answer immediately

---

## Character Voice Consistency

Before writing each chapter, consult `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md` for the **Speech Fingerprint** of every character who appears. Each character must sound distinct — vocabulary, sentence length, verbal tics, register.
